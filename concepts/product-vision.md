# Product Vision

The **AI Strategies for Business Transformations** program treats vision as the first pillar of
corporate AI strategy: a comprehensive plan for harnessing AI to drive business outcomes rests on
a clear picture of what the AI-powered — eventually autonomous — enterprise looks like three to
five years out, the business outcomes it wants to achieve, and where to focus resources.[^p1-product-vision][^r-business-department]
That vision then cascades through initiatives (use cases and data), technology (platforms and
infrastructure), and people (talent, organization, and governance), each answering three guiding
questions. Where-to-play choices follow from sources of advantage, above all the richness of
proprietary data mapped across the customer, operations, administrative, and risk quadrants of the
AI Radar 2.0.[^p1-product-vision][^r-software-system] This requires viewing the enterprise as a value chain — front
office (customers), operations, and support functions — so the vision is grounded in the
processes AI will actually enhance.[^p1-product-vision][^r-business-department]

The **AI-Driven Product Strategy** program applies the same logic at the product level. Product
vision is the North Star: an articulation of the end state, who the product or company aims to
become, and the value delivered to users and stakeholders, aligned by contrast with the product
mission, which describes the journey of what the team does today to reach that destination.[^p2-product-vision][^r-persona]
A good vision answers three questions — the future to create, the problem being solved and why it
matters, and who benefits and how their lives change — and passes six tests: it is ambitious
(enough to seem impossible in the short run), inspiring, unique, enduring, vivid, and easy to
explain.[^p2-product-vision][^r-persona] Strategy then translates customer problems into products that are
desirable, feasible, and viable, cast into execution via frameworks such as V2MOM and the Product
Strategy Canvas, whose layers run from vision, mission, and values down through arenas, bets, and
competencies to metrics and hurdles.[^p2-product-vision][^r-software-system][^r-business-department]

The two programs converge on vision as the alignment device that anchors all downstream
decision-making and prioritization. The C-suite's "AI-powered enterprise" is the enterprise-level
rendition of the product manager's North Star, and both insist the vision stay problem- rather
than solution-focused: the programs warn against product myopia — falling in love with the product
and losing sight of the customer problem — and reframe the product manager as a problem manager,
CEО-of-the-product, customer advocate, and bridge builder across functions.[^p2-product-vision][^r-persona][^r-machine-learning-algorithm][^r-software-system]

The practical takeaway in the AI era is that vision does double duty. It describes the end state
of products that are increasingly intelligent, adaptive, and personalized, and it guides how the
product manager works: shifting from producing analysis and artifacts to orchestrating outcomes,
and building emerging competencies such as prompt engineering, model literacy, agent design, data
product thinking, and ethical oversight.[^p2-product-vision][^r-persona][^r-machine-learning-algorithm][^r-software-system] Professionally, a product vision
should be stated on one page, be aspirational yet concrete, and always trace from the future state
back to the problem that makes it matter — whether that future state is a product or the whole
enterprise.
